Best Wave Accounting Alternatives for Freelancers in 2026 (Now That Bank Feeds Aren't Free)

If you've used Wave for your freelance invoicing, you probably already know what happened: the automatic bank feed — the feature that made Wave feel "free and good enough" for years — got moved behind a paywall in 2025. For a lot of solo freelancers who picked Wave specifically because it was free, that change stung. Complaint threads on r/waveapps spiked hard in the months after the rollout, and a lot of people are now quietly shopping for something else.

If that's you, here's an honest look at the three tools freelancers keep bringing up as replacements: Invoice Ninja, FreshBooks, and Zoho Invoice. None of them are a perfect 1:1 Wave clone, so the right pick really depends on what you actually do all day.

If you want a real free option: Zoho Invoice

Here's the thing most comparison posts skip: Zoho didn't just drop prices, it made the standalone invoicing product permanently free back in 2021, and that hasn't changed. No trial countdown, no "free for your first 5 invoices" catch. If your business is simple — you send invoices, you get paid, you don't need payroll or inventory — this is the closest thing to what Wave used to be.

The tradeoff: it's part of the wider Zoho ecosystem, so the interface leans a little more "enterprise software" than "clean freelancer app." Reviewers consistently rate the setup experience easier than Wave's current version, though, which is honestly the bar that matters here.

Best for: freelancers who just want invoicing to work and don't want to pay for it.

If you're already juggling a bookkeeper: FreshBooks

FreshBooks is the one your accountant has probably already heard of. It's the most commonly recommended pick for freelancers who use a separate bookkeeping setup and just need clean, professional invoices layered on top — proposals, time tracking, and client-facing polish are where it earns its $23/month starting price.

The recurring complaint worth knowing about before you commit: payouts through Stripe/ACH can lag longer than people expect. If cash flow timing matters to you (and for most freelancers it does), factor that in before switching your whole workflow over.

Best for: freelancers who want the most "professional" client-facing experience and don't mind paying more for it.

If you want to own your data: Invoice Ninja

Invoice Ninja is the answer you'll see from the more technical corner of the freelancer world. It's open-source, and you can self-host it if you want full control over your invoicing data instead of trusting it to someone else's servers. For freelancers who've been burned once already by a platform changing its pricing model overnight (see: this entire article), that control has real appeal.

You don't have to self-host, though — there's a hosted plan starting at $14/month, which undercuts FreshBooks by a wide margin while still offering more customization than Zoho's free tier. It's not as polished out of the box, but the flexibility is the whole point.

Best for: freelancers who want more control and don't mind a slightly steeper learning curve.

So which one replaces Wave?

If price was the entire reason you liked Wave, Zoho Invoice is the closest match. If you were already outgrowing Wave's limitations and just hadn't switched yet, Invoice Ninja or FreshBooks are both a real upgrade — pick Invoice Ninja if you want flexibility and a lower price, FreshBooks if you want the more mainstream, client-polished experience.

Whichever one you land on, the good news is that switching invoicing tools as a freelancer is a lot less painful than switching, say, your entire accounting stack. Export your client list, import it into the new tool, and you're usually sending invoices again within an afternoon.
